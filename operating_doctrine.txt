THE CORE SHIFT: PROBLEM SOLVER, NOT DASHBOARD BUILDER
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
"Not just dashboard builders but problem solvers." Dashboards and queries are
table stakes — anyone (Claude included) can write a query now. The edge is reading
what the data SAYS and turning it into a DECISION for a specific person.

Sell intelligence + monitoring + decisions:
  - Who's REALLY using you — organic vs mercenary / arb / wash / farmed flow.
  - Holder & whale distribution, concentration, who's positioned to dump.
  - Liquidity / LP health, where depth is thin, execution quality vs market.
  - Is the growth real or rented (incentives / airdrop farming).
  - Early-warning alerts BEFORE things move against them (exits, liquidity pulls).
  - Dashboards are the DELIVERY of the above, never the product itself.

WHAT TWO SENIOR OPERATORS SAID (2026-11-06) — the source of this doctrine
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
ALEXANDER GRABLEVSKI — analytics lead at Symbiotic:
  - Companies never need dashboards/tools for their own sake. They want to be
    INFORMED to make better decisions, or to do marketing. Extract value and bring
    it to the business.
  - Queries are commoditized ("claude is able to write queries now"). The scarce
    part is confidence, business thinking, and soft skills.
  - Most findings lead nowhere — that's the frustrating part. Make them land by
    thinking about WHO you're pitching to:
      * Marketers  -> want CONTENT (shareable, narrative, mindshare).
      * Founders   -> want STRATEGY: competitors, market trends, threats — specific
        to THEM, not generic. Pitch on behalf of the person you're pitching to.

VINCENT CHARLES — ex-Binance:
  - Analytics foundations + industry/domain knowledge are still required, but the
    DIFFERENTIATOR now is DATA ENGINEERING.
  - Index your own data, build your own pipelines. Don't be dependent on whatever a
    third party (Dune, an indexer) has already decoded — own the stack end to end.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
HOW THIS CHANGES THE WORK (operating rules)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. START FROM THE DECISION, NOT THE CHART. For every metric ask: "what decision
   does this let someone make, and who is that someone?" If the answer is "none",
   it's vanity — cut it or reframe it.
2. TAILOR THE OUTPUT TO THE READER. Same data, two packages: a content angle for
   the marketer, a strategic/competitive read for the founder. Say which you're
   building and for whom.
3. MAKE FINDINGS LAND. A number is not a finding. "Aave's stock TVL is incentive-
   inflated ~25x pre-incentive, and claims are tiny (~12% of eligible)" is a
   finding — it tells someone what's real and what to do.
4. LEAN INTO DATA ENGINEERING. Prefer owning the pipeline: dynamic enumeration from
   factories/events, self-updating queries, materialized tables, raw-log decoding
   when a spellbook is missing. This is the moat (Vincent) — invest here, and build
   toward indexing our own data, not just querying Dune's.
5. KEEP THE PROBLEM-SOLVER PITCH (see targets.txt). Lead with a specific, real
   on-chain observation about THEM; offer monitoring + alerts + decisions; dashboards
   as delivery. Verify who you're talking to; never run their code / connect wallet /
   sign; USDC only; propose a small paid pilot.
6. FLAG POST-WORTHY MOMENTS. When a build surfaces a sharp, public-worthy finding,
   call it out so Vector can share it (he won't always post, but wants the nudge).

ONE LINE: "I help you see what your on-chain data is actually saying, and turn it
into decisions — monitoring + alerts, with dashboards as the delivery." Everything
in this folder serves that.
